# Market research — what the category does today

Researched 2026-11-14 via vendor sites, help centres and review aggregators. Purpose:
borrow the vocabulary and the table stakes, decide what PayLens does and does not do,
and be able to talk about the market in the interview.

## The category in one paragraph

"Compensation management software" has split into three kinds of product: (1) purpose-built
compensation platforms (Pave, Ravio, Figures, Aeqium, beqom, Compport, CompUp) that own
benchmarking data, pay bands and the merit cycle; (2) HCM suites with a comp module
(Workday, SAP SuccessFactors, Dayforce, UKG, HiBob, Lattice, Personio); (3) pay-equity
specialists (Trusaic PayParity, Brightmine, Syndio). Almost all position themselves as the
exit from spreadsheets. Reviewers on G2 most value ease of use, salary management, market
data and support; the recurring complaints are learning curve, missing features, reporting
issues, slow loading and slow performance.

## Notes per vendor (for interview talking points)

- **Pave** — "complete operating system for compensation": benchmark pay in real time, price
  jobs, manage pay ranges, run merit cycles, communicate total rewards; Employee Insights
  shows placement within bands and flags people outside them. Reviewers praise taking merit
  cycles out of spreadsheets; some want deeper benchmarks.
- **Ravio** — Europe-first, real-time benchmarking from 1,800+ tech companies, salary bands,
  pay-equity analysis and EU Pay Transparency Directive reporting; sells "compliance" hard.
- **Lattice Compensation** — bands, cycles with budgets and approvals, analytics and
  pay-equity insights, Mercer benchmarks; recent updates: edit recommendations inline with
  bands/ratings/budget visible; a Data Check page to find and fix inconsistencies before a cycle.
- **Figures** — bands + reviews; explicit about spreadsheet failure modes (version chaos,
  silent formula errors, no audit trail).
- **Aeqium** — merit/bonus/equity cycles, band history with audit trail, pay equity by
  gender/role/department/location, AI that configures a cycle from plain-language requests.
- **beqom / Compport** — enterprise, multi-country, rule engines, audit-ready governance;
  Compport claims pay-equity compliance across 37+ countries.
- **Pay-equity specialists** (Trusaic, Brightmine) — regression-based adjusted gaps,
  intersectional analysis, jurisdiction reports. PayLens does the unadjusted mean/median
  gap and says so; adjusted (regression) gaps are v2.

## Regulatory driver: EU Pay Transparency Directive

- Transposition deadline 7 June 2026; core rights apply to all EU employers: salary ranges
  before interview, no salary-history questions, employees may request pay information,
  gender-neutral pay structures.
- Gender pay gap reporting for employers with 100+ workers, phased: 250+ report by
  7 June 2027 (annual); 150–249 by 7 June 2027 (every 3 years); 100–149 by 7 June 2031.
- Reports include mean and median gender pay gap and bonus gap; a gap ≥ 5% in any
  category that cannot be justified and is not remedied within six months triggers a joint
  pay assessment. Burden of proof shifts to non-compliant employers.
- Implication for PayLens: categories of "equal value" (we use job level × job family as a
  proxy), mean and median gaps per category, and defensible, audited data are v1 concerns.

## What this means for PayLens

1. Table stakes we must have: record + history + audit, bands + compa-ratio, multi-currency,
   analytics with export, import from spreadsheets, roles.
2. Our differentiators within the assessment: answers with their working, the band bar
   everywhere, a natural-language assistant that is tool-grounded and evaluated, and a
   5-minute setup for reviewers.
3. What we loudly leave to v2: cycles, benchmarking, total rewards, integrations.
